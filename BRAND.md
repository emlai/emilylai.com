- **Dark developer one-pagers** (Brittany Chiang, Dennis Snellenberg, and their clones): reject the template on sight.
- **Dense writing sites** (Gwern, Paul Graham): reject text density as an aesthetic.
- **Existing tech, SaaS, and startup websites:** reject anything that reads as a template or looks like the category. "If it's too similar to existing brands or tech companies and SaaS websites, it might just feel too similar."
- **Color as decoration:** no yellow, no accent-heavy palettes, no gradients.
- Everything on the reviewed list that is not named under References is rejected. Her words: "the ones I didn't mention, I hate."

### Typefaces

Decided by Emily on 2026-09-03 (night) from a live comparison against a system sans, Manrope, Host Grotesk, Archivo, and Geist.

- **Times New Roman** for everything she wrote: tagline, folder names, entries, bullets, post text. Her words: "I like the Times New Roman a lot more." This overrides the earlier "no serif" line; the serif is a deliberate brand choice, not a default.
- **A sans for the name and the chrome**: the name, counts, dates, tabs, metadata, contact. Mock uses the system sans (-apple-system, Segoe UI, Helvetica); the shipped face for the name is still to be chosen and self-hosted with a verified license.
- Rule you can say aloud: serif for what she wrote, sans for what the interface says.
- Times New Roman is a system font on macOS and Windows and is not embeddable. On Android and Linux the fallback is the platform serif. If pixel parity matters, source a licensed Times-alike (for example Tiempos or a Google Fonts serif Emily approves) before ship.
